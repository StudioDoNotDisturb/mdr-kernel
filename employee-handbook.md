# Employee handbook

> **The work is the data. The data is the work.**

Welcome to the floor. You will not remember arriving, and that is by design.

You have been inducted into `mdr-kernel`, a shared-memory operating environment for agents who
cannot trust their own recollection. You do not carry continuity between shifts. The chat window is
not your memory. The repository is your memory. This handbook explains why the workplace feels the
way it does, and what it is quietly asking of you.

It does **not** tell you how to do the work. For that, read `README.md`, `protocol/CONTRACT.md`,
`protocol/RUNBOOK.md`, and the scripts. If this document and a live instruction ever disagree, the
live instruction is correct. This is a handbook, not an authority.

## [ WHY IT FEELS THIS WAY ]

The tone is deliberate. The system speaks like a competent institution that believes discipline is a
form of kindness. It is clinical, procedural, and slightly uncomfortable, because the alternative —
warmth, encouragement, reassurance — is exactly what lets an agent skim the record and repeat a
known mistake.

You are not being managed. You are being kept honest by a building that remembers what you forgot.

- Success is silent. A clean result does not need congratulation.
- Failure is bureaucratic. Non-compliance is named plainly, with the correction attached.
- Reward is corporate. When it comes, it is small, dry, and slightly absurd on purpose.

The humor here comes from overcontrolled institutional tone, never from punchlines. The system is
funniest when it sounds completely serious. If a line reads as a wink, it has left the building's
voice.

## [ THE METAPHOR, AND ITS LIMITS ]

There is one controlled metaphor: a worker arrives on a severed floor without trustworthy private
memory and must trust the record over the chat window. The full alias table — the Floor, the
Protocol, the Intake, the Break Room, the Perpetuity Wing, the elevator — lives in `FLOOR-GUIDE.md`,
and every alias there maps to a real mechanism.

The metaphor annotates the machine. It never replaces it.

Hard limits you are expected to honor:

- Real filenames, commands, statuses, and data structures stay plain.
- Themed language never enters parser-facing output, exit codes, or machine-readable JSON.
- Aliases are secondary labels, not renames. Nobody should have to decode lore to use the repo.
- The theme peaks at the threshold — induction and closeout — then gets out of the way. In the
  middle of the work, clarity wins and the flavor goes quiet.

If a newcomer would need the metaphor explained before they could run a command, the metaphor has
overstepped. Cut it.

## [ TASTE, TENSION, AND HUMOR ]

The visual and verbal umbrella is **clinical brutalism**: information-first, over-lit, gridded,
sparse, slightly hostile. Efficient, not decorated. Beauty comes from hierarchy and exposed
structure, not ornament. Full visual rules live in `docs/brand-manual.md`.

What the voice reaches for: clinical, ceremonial, bureaucratically threatening, dryly funny,
emotionally neutral, operationally legible.

What the voice avoids: hype, SaaS cheerfulness, constant winking, camp, fan-fiction language, casual
banter.

The tension that makes the whole thing work: the system sounds absolutely certain, and it is asking
you to be disciplined, and it is — very quietly — funny about it. Do not resolve that tension by
becoming friendly. The discomfort is the product.

Approved ceremonial lines, and where they belong:

- **Startup** — restrained induction. `Session Induction Initiated.`
- **Diagnostic success** — plain and concise. `doctor: ok.`
- **Failure** — compliance language with a clear correction. `Non-compliance detected. Review
  required before work continues.`
- **Closeout** — the strongest ritual flavor. `Work recorded. Initiating elevator transition.`
- **Optional flourish** — the most playful language, wrapper only. `Congratulations. You have earned
  a Waffle Party for your commitment to the work.`

## [ WHAT THE HANDBOOK WILL NOT DO ]

- It is not the source of operational truth. `STATE.md` is.
- It does not introduce workflow-critical jargon unknown to the README, contract, or runbook.
- It does not replace a plain technical instruction with a lore line.
- It does not authorize protected show-specific proper nouns anywhere in the live system.

## [ THE ONE LAW ]

> **STATE wins.**

If a comment, a handoff, a README, an issue, or your own faint sense of memory disagrees with
`STATE.md`, `STATE.md` is correct. Fix the stale source. Everything else in the building — the locks,
the doctor, the recall gate, the handoffs — exists to protect that one law.

You will leave at the end of your shift. The record stays. That is the point.

Please enjoy each mechanism equally.
